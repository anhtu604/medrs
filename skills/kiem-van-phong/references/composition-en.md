# English medical academic composition

Use one controlling topic per paragraph and open with a sentence that states the result, question, or argumentative move. Prefer specific subjects, verbs, outcomes, estimates, and conditions over abstract importance. Use active voice when it clarifies responsibility; retain passive voice when the actor is immaterial or the target convention favors the procedure.

Keep related words together, coordinate comparable ideas in parallel form, maintain deliberate tense, and place emphasis where the scientific contrast belongs. Remove words that add no qualification or logical relation. Do not sacrifice necessary uncertainty, definitions, limitations, or reproducibility detail merely to shorten a sentence.

These principles adapt the public-domain 1918 *Elements of Style*, repository commit `05fc4f0d2b97b7c042dd9949ad658568e4a1324e`, accessed 2026-08-31. They are applied to medical evidence and target-journal conventions, not treated as inflexible grammar laws. Preserve numbers, citations, markers, and scientific meaning while editing.
