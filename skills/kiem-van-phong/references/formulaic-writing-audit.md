# Formulaic-writing quality audit

Use this as a diagnostic field guide, not an authorship test. A pattern is a prompt to inspect evidence, specificity, logic, and voice. It is not proof that AI produced the text, and deleting surface features to evade detection is not a valid revision goal.

Inspect for generic significance or promotional tone; vague attribution; superficial participial explanation; mechanical groups of three; repeated “not only … but also” or similar negative parallelism; canned challenges/future-prospects endings; excessive headings, boldface, lists, or em dashes; and templated language addressed to the user rather than the paper's reader.

For each flag, identify the deeper defect. Replace vague importance with the exact finding and bounded implication. Replace unsupported interpretation with evidence or an unresolved marker. Collapse redundant scaffolding only when the logical relation remains clear. Keep a flagged construction when it is the clearest accurate wording.

Source basis: Wikipedia advice page “Signs of AI writing”, revision `1372013638` dated 2026-08-29, accessed 2026-08-31, CC BY-SA. The page itself describes the list as non-prescriptive, warns that signs are not proof, and advises addressing deeper problems rather than merely hiding indicators. This adaptation uses categories and caveats without copying its examples.
