# GRADE-CERQual confidence contract

CERQual evaluates confidence in an individual finding from a qualitative evidence synthesis. Define the finding precisely and preserve the contributing-study map. Assess four components: methodological limitations of contributing studies, coherence of the finding, adequacy of supporting data, and relevance to the review question. A global label for the entire qualitative review is not a substitute.

For each component, give the concern level, explanation, and traceable evidence. Methodological limitations draw on an appropriate qualitative appraisal of contributing studies but are interpreted for this finding. Coherence asks whether the data convincingly support the explanatory pattern, including variation or disconfirming cases. Adequacy considers richness and quantity, not sample count alone. Relevance compares contributing evidence with the review context, population, phenomenon and setting.

Form the overall confidence judgment transparently from the four component assessments and explain any downgrading. Missing contributing-study links, unsupported summaries or absent component evidence result in `UNRESOLVED`. Do not create a High-confidence finding merely because all studies point in the same direction.

The coverage manifest identifies the four components and the official CERQual guidance landing page. Because the official materials carry their own rights and versions, this reference offers an independent operational summary rather than reproducing worksheets. Record the guidance version actually used, verification date, component judgments, overall confidence, explanation and review-team approval state.
