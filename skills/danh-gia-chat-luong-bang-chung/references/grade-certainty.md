# GRADE certainty contract

GRADE addresses certainty in a body of evidence for a defined outcome, comparison and context. Establish the starting point appropriate to the evidence design, then explicitly assess risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision and publication bias. A randomized design does not automatically finish at High certainty: every domain still needs outcome-specific evidence and explanation.

For observational evidence in intervention questions, upgrading considerations may include a large effect, a plausible residual-confounding pattern that would reduce the observed effect, or a dose-response gradient. Apply them only after the relevant downgrade assessment and only when supported. Do not double count the same limitation across domains or upgrade on the basis of statistical significance.

Report the initial certainty, each domain judgment, direction and magnitude of any rating change, rationale with source locator, and final category. Missing domain evidence yields `UNRESOLVED`; it must not silently become “not serious.” Connect imprecision to the decision threshold and information size, inconsistency to effect distributions and explanations, and indirectness to the exact population/intervention/comparator/outcome question.

The current implementation profile is the online Cochrane Handbook 6.5, Chapter 14, accessed 2026-09-02. Recheck the official source when the verification banner expires. If a panel uses a different official GRADE guidance edition, record it and do not combine incompatible rules without a declared deviation.
