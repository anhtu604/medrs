# JBI analytical cross-sectional and prevalence appraisal

Load for analytical cross-sectional association studies or prevalence estimates after identifying the appraisal target. These are different instruments. The canonical manifests are `../../../coverage/jbi-analytical-2026.yaml` and `../../../coverage/jbi-prevalence-current-2026.yaml`. Their item counts and locators were checked against official downloaded DOCX files on 2026-10-11; exact hashes are recorded in the manifests. Original files remain in private source history, not the redistributable package.

## Source versions, rights and use-time requirements

The analytical instrument is the revised 2026 version linked by JBI, with the corresponding publication in JBI Evidence Synthesis 24(3):401–408. Its footer cites a 2025 DOI reference; record that discrepancy rather than label the file a legacy instrument. The prevalence DOCX has no explicit revision date in its title and cites a 2015 paper. Retrieval in 2026 is not a claim that the prevalence instrument was revised in 2026. Manifest IDs map to the actual current file, not remembered numbering from an earlier tool.

Source: https://jbi.global/critical-appraisal-tools. Copyright belongs to JBI; no redistribution grant is inferred from a download link. MedRS distributes independently written navigation and provenance, not an official translation or copied workbook. Obtain and load the official instrument under its applicable terms at use time. An inaccessible link or absent local original means `OFFICIAL_TOOL_REQUIRED`; item navigation alone does not certify a completed official appraisal. Offline use may load an author-provided matching original and record its hash. Recheck after the 365-day verification interval, on a source update, or when the protocol pins another version.

## Analytical cross-sectional workflow

Define the exposure, outcome, comparison, timepoint and specific estimate. The eight items cover eligibility, condition definition, exposure measurement, outcome measurement, confounder identification, confounding strategy, statistical analysis, and actual participants/setting. Load each named source locator from the manifest and complete the official response set rather than treat those topic names as answers.

The revised instrument distinguishes assessment levels. Items 1, 3, 5 and 8 address the study; items 2, 4 and 6 require outcome-specific assessment; item 7 concerns the particular result. Do not reuse an answer about one outcome's diagnosis or adjustment model for another outcome just because both belong to the same paper. Participant description is a reporting concern and must not silently become a causal-validity domain. Document judgments with methods/results/supplement locators, not an abstract alone.

## Prevalence workflow

Define the target population, sampling frame, condition definition, geographical period and prevalence numerator/denominator. Complete all nine item IDs, preserving unclear/not-applicable responses where the official rules permit them. Check sample representation and subgroup coverage separately from overall sample size or response rate. Precision-based adequacy is not a universal fixed minimum N. Different case definitions or ascertainment procedures can make pooled estimates non-comparable even when the denominators match.

## Evidence and handoff

Use two appraisers where the review protocol requires them; an AI self-check is not two independent human reviewers. Keep first judgments, disagreements, resolution and author decisions in dated history. If the protocol uses a prespecified exclusion rule, record it before seeing convenient results; do not invent a numerical total-score cutoff or remove studies merely to improve the pooled result.

Return instrument/version/hash, target, all item responses, source evidence and rationale, assessment levels, unresolved information and the actual official-tool usage record. Keep appraisal separate from STROBE completeness and GRADE outcome-level certainty. Hand unresolved items to synthesis as limitations/sensitivity requirements; never replace missing evidence with a low-risk label. A manifest passing structural validation only proves internal item coverage, not source fidelity, clinical judgment, current access or final appraisal correctness.
