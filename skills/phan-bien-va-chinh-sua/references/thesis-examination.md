# Thesis examination

Examine the submitted thesis as an independent academic artifact. State scope and version, then assess question and contribution, design, sampling, measurements, analysis, results, interpretation, literature use, ethics/data governance, reporting integrity, structure and presentation. Tie each finding to a page, section, table or figure and explain its consequence.

Distinguish issues that threaten validity from correctable reporting or presentation defects. Check alignment among objectives, Methods, Results, Discussion and conclusions. Do not infer misconduct from an error or formulaic prose; record evidence and request clarification. Do not rewrite the thesis while acting as examiner.

Return an overall recommendation only after listing its governing findings and any institution-specific decision categories. If the official examination rubric or decision labels are unavailable, return `OFFICIAL_RULE_REQUIRED` for the formal verdict while still providing a clearly labelled scholarly review. Questions for the defense should test unresolved reasoning, not ambush the candidate with facts outside the thesis scope.
